Knee pain is one of the most common problems affecting people of different age groups. Whether it is difficulty climbing stairs, discomfort while walking, stiffness after sitting for long periods, or persistent joint pain, knee problems can significantly affect everyday life.

Many people ignore early symptoms until the pain becomes severe and starts interfering with their mobility. However, identifying the cause of knee pain early and following an appropriate treatment plan can help improve joint function, mobility, and overall quality of life.

What Causes Knee Pain?

Knee pain can develop due to several factors, including ageing, injuries, lifestyle habits, and underlying joint conditions.

Some common causes include:

1. Osteoarthritis

Osteoarthritis occurs when the protective cartilage cushioning the knee joint gradually wears down. This can cause pain, stiffness, swelling, and difficulty moving the knee.

2. Muscle Weakness

Weak muscles around the knee, particularly the quadriceps and hamstrings, can affect joint stability and increase stress on the knee during everyday activities.

3. Ligament and Meniscus Injuries

Sports injuries, sudden twisting movements, and accidents can damage the ligaments or meniscus, resulting in pain, swelling, and restricted movement.

4. Excess Body Weight

Additional body weight increases the mechanical load on the knee joints, potentially contributing to discomfort and worsening existing knee problems.

5. Lack of Physical Activity

Prolonged sitting and limited movement can contribute to muscle weakness, joint stiffness, and reduced flexibility.

Common Symptoms You Should Not Ignore

Knee problems do not always begin with severe pain. Some early warning signs include:

  • Pain while walking or climbing stairs
  • Stiffness after waking up
  • Swelling around the knee joint
  • Difficulty sitting down or standing up
  • Reduced knee flexibility
  • Pain after prolonged standing
  • A feeling of instability while walking

If these symptoms persist or worsen, professional assessment can help identify the underlying problem.

Can Knee Pain Be Managed Without Surgery?

Not every knee problem requires surgery. Depending on the underlying condition and its severity, several conservative treatment approaches may help manage symptoms and improve mobility.

Some commonly used approaches include:

Physiotherapy: Targeted exercises can strengthen the muscles supporting the knee, improve flexibility, and help restore functional movement.

Naturopathy: Selected naturopathic therapies may complement a broader wellness and pain-management programme.

Joint Mobility Exercises: Guided movement exercises can help improve flexibility and reduce stiffness.

Lifestyle Modifications: Maintaining a healthy weight, improving posture, and following appropriate physical activity routines can support joint health.

Personalised Rehabilitation: A treatment programme based on an individual's symptoms, physical condition, and mobility goals can support recovery.

The appropriate approach depends on a proper clinical assessment. Surgery may still be necessary in certain advanced or complex conditions.

Why Is Physiotherapy Important for Knee Pain?

Physiotherapy plays an important role in managing many musculoskeletal conditions.

A personalised physiotherapy programme may include:

  • Strengthening exercises for supporting muscles
  • Flexibility and mobility training
  • Movement and posture correction
  • Functional rehabilitation
  • Guided exercises for improving balance and stability

Consistent rehabilitation can help patients regain confidence in everyday movements and improve their ability to perform routine activities.

When Should You Consult a Knee Pain Specialist?

You should consider professional evaluation if:

  • Knee pain continues for several days or keeps returning.
  • Walking becomes uncomfortable or difficult.
  • You experience recurring swelling or stiffness.
  • Knee movement becomes restricted.
  • Pain interferes with your daily routine.

Seek urgent medical attention if you cannot bear weight, have a severely swollen or deformed knee, or develop a hot, red knee with fever.

Early assessment can help prevent unnecessary delays in diagnosis and treatment.

2. Can physiotherapy help with osteoarthritis knee pain?

Physiotherapy may help improve muscle strength, joint mobility, flexibility, and everyday function in people with knee osteoarthritis.

3. Is knee pain always a sign of arthritis?

No. Knee pain can also result from muscle weakness, ligament injuries, meniscus damage, overuse, and other musculoskeletal conditions.

4. Can knee pain be treated without surgery?
